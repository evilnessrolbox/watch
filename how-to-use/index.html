How to use Audio Studio

A free audio editor that runs in your browser. Change speed and pitch, cut a section, play it backwards, then save it as WAV, MP3 or OGG. No sign-up and no software to install.

Step by step

1. Add your audio. Drag a file onto the box on the main page, or click the box to choose one. MP3, WAV, OGG and M4A files work. The waveform appears when it has loaded.
2. Press Play. Use Play to listen at any time. It always plays your audio with all your edits applied, so you hear the final result before saving.
3. Pick a tool on the left. You can use as many as you like, and they add up. A small dot on a tool means it's active.
4. Export. Open the Export tab, choose WAV, MP3 or OGG, and press Download.

The tools

Speed

Move the slider from 0.25x (four times slower) to 3x (three times faster). By default the pitch stays the same, so a sped-up voice doesn't sound like a chipmunk. Tick the checkbox if you want speed to change pitch too, like a vinyl record.

Pitch

Raise or lower the pitch by up to 12 semitones (one octave) without changing how long the audio is. Small changes of 1 to 4 semitones sound the cleanest.

Cutter

Drag the two round handles on the waveform, or type exact start and end times in seconds. Only the highlighted part is kept. Press "Reset selection" to start over.

Invert

Turns the audio backwards. It's applied after the cut, so you can reverse just the part you selected.

Tips

  • Combine tools freely, for example cut a chorus, slow it to 0.8x and lower the pitch by 2 semitones.
  • Longer files take a few seconds to process after each change.
  • WAV keeps full quality but makes bigger files. MP3 is smaller and works everywhere.
  • Your original file is never changed. You always save a new copy.

Common questions

Is my audio uploaded anywhere?
No. Everything happens inside your browser on your own device. See the Privacy Policy.

Why is OGG greyed out?
Some browsers can't create OGG files. Firefox can. Use WAV or MP3 in other browsers.

How long can my file be?
There's no fixed limit, but very long files use more memory and take longer to process.
